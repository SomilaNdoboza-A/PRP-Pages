ARTEFACT 05 / DAILY RECORD

Agile Logbook

A structured summary of the final development period, based on the team’s supplied account and the lecturer’s logbook prompts.

Logbook context

The team provided the period 4–7 August as the final days of development after earlier presentations. The team’s summary was that the frontend, backend functionality and database were worked on, and testing was also done.

The entries below are a structured retrospective summary based on that information and the lecturer’s Agile Logbook prompts. They should be checked against the team’s actual records before being presented as contemporaneous daily notes.

Stand-up and taskboard prompts

The lecturer’s template asks the team to consider:

  • What work has advanced since the previous stand-up?
  • What work was completed outside the visible taskboard?
  • What does the team still need to finish together?
  • Were there bottlenecks or blockers?
  • What was completed since the last stand-up, what is planned next, and what impediments need attention?
Evidence to attach: Taskboard screenshot showing Product Backlog, In Progress and Completed; dated stand-up notes or logbook entries; relevant commits; screenshots of the working application; and test evidence.

Personal contribution

My assigned role was Backend and Database Lead. The work I was responsible for included backend/API development, SQLite database design and connectivity, authentication and the connection between frontend functionality and backend APIs. When adding personal examples, describe the specific code, database tables, endpoints or issues you personally handled and link them to commits or screenshots where possible.

Reflection

The final development days taught me that completing a software project requires more than finishing individual tasks. The frontend, backend and database need to work together, and the team needs to test the result rather than assume that separate components will behave correctly once combined. I also saw the value of a logbook: it creates a record of decisions, progress and remaining work. For a stronger future logbook, I would record stand-up updates on the day, include blockers and resolutions, and attach test evidence immediately instead of reconstructing details later.
