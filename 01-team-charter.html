ARTEFACT 01 / TEAM AGREEMENT

Agile Team Charter

The charter establishes why the KasiBook team exists, how responsibilities are shared and what the team considers completed work.

1. Team purpose and vision

Mission: Solve the operational challenges faced by township entrepreneurs and small informal businesses — including barbers, nail technicians, salon owners, kota vendors and mechanics — who currently manage bookings and orders through WhatsApp messages, phone calls and notebooks.

Success definition: Enable business owners to organise daily operations, reduce scheduling conflicts, keep accessible customer records and improve service quality and business growth.

Product: An affordable digital booking and order management platform with an online booking page for each business, a central dashboard for appointments and orders, and customer information storage.

3. Ways of working

The team adopted Scrum with one-week sprints during the development phase. Daily stand-ups were used to share progress, next steps and impediments. Sprint planning was held at the start of a sprint, with a review/demo and retrospective at the end.

Working agreements

  • Arrive prepared and on time for team ceremonies.
  • Respect meeting timeboxes.
  • Give constructive, specific feedback focused on the work.
  • Raise blockers early, no later than the daily stand-up after identifying them.
  • Use retrospective actions to improve the next sprint.

Communication

WhatsApp was used for urgent and day-to-day coordination; email for formal documentation and lecturer correspondence; and in-person or virtual meetings for sprint ceremonies and design reviews.

4. Decision-making and Definition of Done

Where the team could not reach agreement on a technical or design decision within one working day, the charter specified a team vote, with a two-out-of-three majority. Issues affecting project scope or lecturer requirements were to be escalated to the lecturer.

The charter’s Definition of Done covered code completion and peer review, unit tests, UI alignment with approved designs, API documentation and testing, frontend/backend integration verification, QA test execution, updated documentation, acceptance criteria and readiness for demonstration.

5. Risks and constraints

  • Potential requirement changes, varying team availability, conflicting feature ideas and delayed feedback.
  • Frontend work depended on backend API specifications and approved UI designs.
  • The project had a fixed academic timeline, no commercial budget and a requirement to use an accessible technology stack.
Evidence to attach: Original signed/approved Agile Team Charter, team meeting or communication evidence (where permitted), and any updated charter version. The original charter was last updated 29 October 2026.

Reflection

The charter helped make the team’s responsibilities and expectations clearer. My role as Backend and Database Lead was specific, but the charter also showed me that backend work depends on communication with frontend developers and the Scrum Master. Agreeing on how to raise blockers, make decisions and define completed work reduced the risk of each person working in isolation. I also learned that a Definition of Done should include testing, documentation and integration, not only code that appears to work on one developer’s machine.
